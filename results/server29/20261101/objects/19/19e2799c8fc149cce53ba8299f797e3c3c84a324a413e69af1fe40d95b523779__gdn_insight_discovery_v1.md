## Section 3: Discovery Results

### A. Bit-Width Behavior

**Observation:** Per-row quantization from 8 to 3 bits did not show numerical failure through 1024 generated tokens, but token agreement and reconstruction error degrade non-monotonically.

**Example:** 8-bit mean error `0.0092`, agreement `0.2939`; 6-bit mean error `0.0244`, agreement `0.4688`; 4-bit mean error `0.0493`, agreement `0.0518`; 3-bit mean error `0.0551`, agreement `0.0557`. No NaN/Inf or first failure was observed.

**Hypothesis:** With per-row scaling, bit width alone may not determine stability up to 1024 tokens. The quality/stability transition may depend on interaction among bit width, prompt trajectory, and scale orientation.

**Evidence strength:** moderate for "no 1024-token numerical phase transition under per-row"; weak for quality conclusions.

**Alternative explanations:** Single prompt, free generation path, and greedy divergence can make token agreement noisy. The per-row setting was selected because earlier runs already showed it is stable.

**Next verification:** Focus on 5/4/3-bit per-row at 4096 tokens on multiple prompts, plus aligned logit KL.

**Do not conclude:** Do not claim 3-bit or 4-bit is generally safe.

### B. Controlled Perturbation Geometry

**Observation:** Same-norm one-time perturbations propagate differently depending on geometry and layer. Most perturbations were damped, but damping rate varies strongly.

**Example:** final/start divergence ratios: single-head layer 25 `0.815`; column-sparse layer 9 `0.545`; iid layer 9 `0.540`; rank-1 layer 9 `0.536`; row-sparse layer 25 `0.421`; single-head layer 9 almost vanished at `4.63e-7`.

**Hypothesis:** GDN recurrent dynamics are not only sensitive to perturbation magnitude. Error geometry and layer location affect whether state error is preserved or damped.

**Evidence strength:** moderate.

**Alternative explanations:** Only one prompt, two target layers, and synthetic 5% state-norm perturbations were used. The perturbed state may not match real quantization residual geometry.

**Next verification:** Repeat the most separated cases across layers `1,9,17,25,29`, at two perturbation magnitudes, and compare with actual INT4-row/column residual directions.

**Do not conclude:** Do not design geometry-aware quantization yet.

### C. Quantization Cadence

**Observation:** Increasing the interval between INT4-row fake quantization events reduces observed reconstruction error, but token agreement does not improve monotonically.

**Example:** K=1 mean error `0.0595`, agreement `0.1035`; K=4 mean error `0.0318`, agreement `0.1563`; K=32 mean error `0.0055`, agreement `0.1973`. No first failure through 512 tokens.

**Hypothesis:** Error injection frequency is likely a real variable, but reconstruction error alone is insufficient to predict generation quality.

**Evidence strength:** weak-to-moderate.

**Alternative explanations:** For K>1, non-quantized steps record observed state with zero reconstruction error, so the mean error partially reflects fewer injection events. Free-generation agreement is also affected by early token divergence.

**Next verification:** Add teacher-forced FP32-path state/logit divergence for K=1,4,16,32.

**Do not conclude:** Do not package K-token cadence as an algorithm.

### D. Prefill vs Decode

**Observation:** Current fake quant is applied after the full prompt forward returns `past_key_values`, then after each one-token decode forward. It is not applied inside each recurrent update during prefill.

**Example:** status: `PREFILL_RECURRENT_FAKE_QUANT_BLOCKED_BY_CHUNKWISE_KERNEL`.

**Hypothesis:** Prefill state construction and decode recurrent feedback may have different sensitivity, but current instrumentation can only test chunk-end prefill quantization and decode-step quantization.

**Evidence strength:** strong for audit; none for prefill/decode sensitivity itself.

**Alternative explanations:** The limitation is instrumentation-level, not necessarily model-level.

**Next verification:** Run feasible modes: FP32 prefill + INT4 decode, final prefill state quantize once + FP32 decode, FP32 prefill + INT4 decode.

**Do not conclude:** Do not claim prefill is insensitive or sensitive yet.

### E. Outlier Persistence

**Observation:** FP32 recurrent states show persistent hot heads/rows/columns across sampled time points.

**Example:** mean top-10 Jaccard for layer 1: head `0.784`, row `0.836`, column `0.886`; layer 9: head `0.745`, row `0.751`, column `0.806`; layer 29: head `0.692`, row `0.629`, column `0.749`.

**Hypothesis:** Persistent hot directions may explain why quantization scale orientation affects long-decode stability.

**Evidence strength:** moderate.

**Alternative explanations:** Max-abs top-k can overemphasize stable coordinates that are not causally important for logits. Single prompt limits generality.

**Next verification:** Repeat top-k persistence across the insight prompt set and compare overlap across prompts.

**Do not conclude:** Do not assume persistent hot rows/columns are the root cause.

### F. Spectral Structure

**Observation:** Sampled FP32 state matrices are often highly low-rank, while INT4-row quantization error is less concentrated but still structured.

**Example:** layer 1 token 128 head 0 state top-1 energy `0.982`, top-2 `0.998`; its quantization error top-1 energy `0.639`, top-16 `0.973`. Layer 25 token 1024 state top-4 energy `0.984`, error top-4 `0.607`, error top-16 `0.858`.

**Hypothesis:** State matrices may have strong low-rank structure, while quantization residuals occupy broader but non-random subspaces. This could influence recurrent propagation.

**Evidence strength:** weak-to-moderate.

**Alternative explanations:** Only head 0 was sampled; hottest heads may have different spectra.

**Next verification:** Sample hottest heads and failure-adjacent layers before/after instability.

**Do not conclude:** Do not claim the whole state or error is globally low-rank.

### G. Rounding Dynamics

**Observation:** In this Stage 1 run, stochastic rounding increased INT4-row reconstruction error compared with nearest rounding.

**Example:** nearest mean error `0.0595`, max `0.3168`; stochastic mean error `0.1337`, max `0.4878`. Neither failed through 512 tokens.

**Hypothesis:** Stochastic rounding may change residual variance in a way that is not automatically beneficial for recurrent feedback.

**Evidence strength:** weak.

**Alternative explanations:** Only one seed was tested; residual signed bias and temporal residual correlation were not yet recorded.

**Next verification:** Run multiple fixed seeds and add signed mean error, RMS error, cosine with state, and temporal residual correlation.

**Do not conclude:** Do not claim stochastic rounding is worse in general.

### H. Hybrid GDN/Full-Attention Propagation

**Observation:** Not run in Stage 1.

**Example:** No teacher-forced hidden-state divergence data generated yet.

**Hypothesis:** Full Attention layers may damp, preserve, or amplify accumulated GDN state divergence.

**Evidence strength:** none.

**Alternative explanations:** Deferred due to runtime cost and need for careful teacher-forced alignment.

**Next verification:** Run FP32 vs INT8-column vs INT4-row hidden divergence on an FP32 token path for 256/512 tokens, marking full attention layers `3,7,11,15,19,23,27,31`.

**Do not conclude:** No claim about Full Attention damping.

# Negative Results

- No numerical failure was observed in 8/7/6/5/4/3-bit per-row through 1024 tokens.
- No cadence setting failed through 512 tokens.
- Stochastic rounding did not improve local reconstruction error in the single-seed run.
- Hybrid Full Attention propagation was not measured yet.
- True per-token prefill fake quant is blocked without modifying internal model/kernel behavior.

# Surprising Results

- 6-bit per-row had higher token agreement than 8-bit per-row in this single free-generation run, despite larger reconstruction error. This reinforces that reconstruction error is not equal to generation quality.
- Single-head perturbation at layer 9 almost vanished, while single-head perturbation at layer 25 persisted much more strongly.
- INT4-row remained stable in prior 8192 granularity ablation and again showed no failure in this 1024-token bitwidth screening.
- Stochastic rounding increased mean reconstruction error in this first diagnostic run.

# Row/Column Status

The row/column granularity insight remains strong, but it is no longer the only promising direction.

Current status:

- INT8-column remains the best known INT8 granularity from prior 8192 stability and 256-token quality screens.
- INT4-row remains the best known INT4 granularity from prior 8192 stability.
- The new discovery run suggests deeper mechanisms to investigate: hot-direction persistence, geometry-dependent damping, and cadence.

# FINAL_RESEARCH_DECISION

`MULTIPLE_PROMISING_INSIGHTS_FOUND`

Reason:

Stage 1 did not replace the row/column finding, but it identified multiple mechanisms with plausible GDN-specific research value:

- error geometry affects recurrent propagation
- persistent hot directions exist in FP32 state
- per-row bitwidth behavior does not collapse smoothly into a simple INT4 failure story
- cadence may separate quantization precision from repeated error injection

No final method should be designed yet. The next step should be selective confirmation of the top-ranked insights.
